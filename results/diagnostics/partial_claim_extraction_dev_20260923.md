# Source-preserving partial-claim development probe — 23 September 2026

A conservative extractor now scans the full paragraph, selects an exact short “X is Y” source span when its structure is simple, and otherwise abstains. It does not assert an ontology relation. The [source-only projection](../../data/benchmarks/fm2-0/chapter1-partial-claim-development-source/sources.jsonl) contains 14 previously reviewed chapter 1 development paragraphs with labels and formulas removed. Its [manifest](../../data/benchmarks/fm2-0/chapter1-partial-claim-development-source/manifest.json) pins the original development packet and candidate source hashes. The production model saw only this projection, never the AI review labels.

The extractor selected three candidates, all from the three AI-reviewed partial-positive paragraphs, and abstained on the other eleven. This is **in-sample behavior on a small, deliberately selected development set**, not a measured extraction accuracy. The full-paragraph scan covered each reviewed source span, including four that occurred after the first sentence. The selected spans are exact substrings of the original paragraphs:

| --- | --- | --- |
| 1-79 | `Open-source intelligence is intelligence` | Abstained: no lexically supported continuation |
| 1-88 | `Technical intelligence is intelligence` | Abstained: no lexically supported continuation |

The [saved CPU run](partial_claim_dev_cpu_20260923_02/predictions.jsonl), [run manifest](partial_claim_dev_cpu_20260923_02/manifest.json), and [separate scoring](partial_claim_dev_cpu_20260923_02/scoring/summary.json) record 11 extraction abstentions, two decoder abstentions, one compile error, zero accepted claims, and zero exact matches to the reviewed formulas. The model checkpoint and source hashes are pinned in the manifests. The run used the project-owned, rootless, network-disabled, CPU-limited container on worker `192.168.94.13` as `save-water`. No training or GPU inference was started; the existing vLLM GPU service remained active.

The two intelligence fragments show a semantic gap: “X is intelligence” alone does not distinguish a produced intelligence **product** from an intelligence discipline. The reviewed formulas use `OpenSourceIntelligenceProduct` and `TechnicalIntelligenceProduct`; the third uses `BiometricsProcess`. None of these sense-specific symbols is in the production closed class vocabulary. The extractor must not invent or admit them automatically from a short surface fragment. A reviewed declaration/sense step and retention of the full evidence sentence are needed before the decoder can be fairly assessed on these partial claims. Even with that step, compilation and source-grounding must remain mandatory, with semantic review before accepting an assertion.

An [initial first-sentence-only run](partial_claim_dev_cpu_20260923/predictions.jsonl) was superseded when four reviewed spans proved to occur later in their paragraphs. It produced the same route counts but did not adequately scan the sources; the full-paragraph run above is the operative result. The extractor is intentionally narrow and may miss sound claims with other grammar. Its punctuation-based sentence splitting can fail on abbreviations, and it takes the first eligible candidate per paragraph. The source-only projection derives its 14 IDs from the reviewed development packet, so its candidate counts are not an independent validation. The chapter 2 packet has already been queried in a prior diagnostic and must not be treated as a fresh final holdout. A future evaluation of a fixed extraction and term-sense procedure needs new, unseen source passages and labels frozen before any model query.
